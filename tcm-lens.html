How It Works

A different question:
not what's broken, but what's out of balance.

A plain-English guide to how Traditional Chinese Medicine actually reads the body, and why it sees the small things modern health advice misses.

Western medicine is brilliant at finding what's broken: a marker out of range, a part that's failed. But a lot of us live in the gap it doesn't have words for: every test is "normal", yet you're tired, bloated, foggy, cold. Off.

Traditional Chinese Medicine lives in exactly that gap. It doesn't wait for something to break. It reads the whole system as it runs day to day, and asks a simpler question: is the energy flowing, and is there enough warmth to move it?

Your core vitality: the warm engine in the middle

At the centre of it all is what we call your core vitality (in the classics, the central qi): the inner fire in your middle that turns food, rest and movement into usable energy. When it's strong, things flow. You digest well, sleep deep, feel warm and clear. When it dims, the whole system slows: food sits heavy, damp builds, you feel sluggish and cold no matter what you eat.

Cold food, iced drinks, skipped breakfasts, late nights: most modern habits quietly put that fire out. Not dramatically. Just a little, every day.

The four ideas that explain almost everything

1 · Warmth vs cold

Digestion is a warm process: your body has to gently "cook" food before it can use it. Cold, raw, fridge-temperature food makes it spend its own warmth first. Do that daily and the fire runs down.

2 · Yin & yang: the cold/slow and the warm/active

Not mystical: just the balance between the still, cooling, slowing side of you and the warm, moving, active side. Pile on the cold and slow, and everything downshifts.

3 · Damp: heavy, stuck, waterlogged

When the system can't move things along, they pool into a heavy, sluggish, "waterlogged" state. That's the puffiness, the brain fog, the constant throat-clearing nobody connects to their lunch.

4 · Flow: everything turning in a circle

Health, in this view, is one constant flow: energy rising, circulating, settling, day and night. Stagnation is when it gets stuck. Most of what we do either keeps that circle turning, or quietly jams it.

Why this is good news

Because almost none of it requires anything extreme. If the problems come from cold, stagnation and a dimmed inner fire, the fixes are warm, simple and daily: cooked over raw, warm over iced, rhythm over chaos, small changes matched to how you're running. That's all YinYang Well really does: read where your core vitality is, and help you bring it back.

In practice

How we work with it

1 · Read your whole picture

A detailed intake + photos of your tongue, face, hands, nails and ears let a senior doctor see where your system runs cold, stuck or depleted.

2 · Say what is actually driving it

Not a label, but the thread: where warmth is running low, where flow is stuck, where damp has piled up, and which of those came first.

3 · Change the daily habits

The small, repeatable shifts that protect your warmth and keep the flow moving. Things you'll actually keep doing.

Which one you need, and why it helps

Everything above says the same thing in different words: symptoms scattered across sleep, digestion, skin and mood usually trace back to one or two things: warmth running low, flow getting stuck, damp piling up. Each of our services works on a different part of that, so it's worth knowing what each one is actually for.

Why you'd start here: you can't change what you can't see. Most people arrive with a list of complaints that feel unrelated: the 3pm crash, the bloating, the cold feet, the waking at 4am, and no idea which are connected. The report answers that. A senior physician at Baoputang Clinic reads your intake and your photographs and tells you which one system is struggling, and what has been draining it.

Observation Reading · £850

Why anyone would go further: the report tells you how your body is running now. The Observation Reading asks the harder question, which is how it got there. She is not part of the Baoputang team and is not a medical doctor.

Why it isn't simply a bigger report: it is a different reader, not a longer version of the same thing. It also answers the one question the report cannot: whether treatment in person would add anything in your case, and honestly how much. That answer is often no, and it is written into the reading either way.
