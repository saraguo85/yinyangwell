What you are buying

This is the reading, not an application to be treated.

Most people who take an Observation Reading never travel, and that is a complete outcome rather than a half one. You are paying for one experienced person's attention and her account of what she sees. If in-person work would help, she says so and says what it would involve. But the reading is not a screening step you have to pass, and there is no verdict at the end about whether you qualify for anything.

Two ways to take it. A live reading usually takes under ten minutes by video, conducted in Chinese with an English interpreter present throughout, and you can stay on longer if you have things you want to ask her. A written account of what she said follows afterwards. She has studied your photographs and intake before you meet, so the time on the call is spent on you rather than on catching up. A written reading skips the call: she works from your photographs and intake alone, and the account comes to you in full. The fee is the same either way, because her time on your case is the same.
Why the fee is what it is. It buys one practitioner's own time on your case, from the first look to the written account, rather than a form processed against a template. The reading itself is short: she looks, and she is quick about it. You are paying for the looking, not for the length of it.
If what you have is pain

Pain is the case this suits best, and the one you can judge for yourself.

Most people who write to us about pain have a route behind them already: scans that were normal, or that showed something nobody wants to operate on. A course of physiotherapy. Injections that held for a while. Painkillers they would rather not keep taking. A waiting list.

She is not restricted to one tool. She works with four things: her hands, needles, scraping and moxa. Which of them, and in what proportion, is decided from your case rather than by what she is set up to offer. That combination is the part that is hard to buy here, where a physiotherapist works with hands and an acupuncturist works with needles, and you choose the room before anyone has looked at you.
The reading takes pain as a history, not a site. When it started and what you were doing then. What has made it better and worse since. What the rest of you has been doing while you have been guarding it. Whether sessions in person would add anything, how many, and what they would cost. If she thinks they would not, she says that, and the reading is still yours.
You will be the one who knows. Nobody here is going to tell you it worked. Pain is one of the few things you can measure without anyone interpreting it for you: write it down, nought to ten, before you start and after each session. If it does not move, you will know before we do.
望 · What she works from

Photographs, and a history long enough to be worth reading.

Traditional Chinese Medicine has four ways of examining a person. Two of them, looking and asking, carry across distance, and this reading is built on those. It does not attempt the two that need you in the room.

01

Photographs, taken to instruction

Tongue, face, hands, nails and ears, shot in daylight to a short written guide we send you. Poor photographs are the single most common reason a reading has to be sent back for retakes.

02

A long intake, not a questionnaire

Sleep, appetite, temperature, thirst, digestion, energy through the day, menstrual history where relevant, and the part most forms leave out: everything you have already tried, in the order you tried it, and what happened each time.

03

Your own account, in your own words

There is a free-text section with no character limit. People routinely tell us this is the first time anyone has asked for the whole story rather than the last six weeks of it.

04

She reads it herself

The same person who writes the report is the person who looks at the photographs. Nothing is triaged by an assistant first.

The report

What is in it, and what is deliberately not.

What the reading contains

  • What she sees in the photographs, described plainly rather than in terminology you would have to look up
  • How your body has been running, in the way TCM reads it: the whole picture rather than a list of complaints
  • Where she thinks this has come from: the living, working and eating that shaped it
  • What to be careful of, and which of your current habits are working against you
  • What she would change first, and in what order: sleep timing, warmth, food, movement, pace
  • Whether she thinks treatment in person would add anything, and honestly how much

Honest limits

When we will say no.

We would rather lose the enquiry than take £850 from someone this cannot help. If any of the following applies, we will say so and we will not take the booking.

  • You have symptoms that need investigating. Unexplained weight loss, bleeding, a lump, chest pain, a change you have noticed and not shown anyone, please see a doctor. That comes first, and a reading is not a reason to wait.
  • You want to know whether you have a particular condition. This reading does not answer that question and is not designed to. Asking it to is the wrong use of it.
  • You are hoping for a cure we cannot promise. Traditional Chinese Medicine is not an alternative to treatment for serious disease, and we do not present it as one.
  • You are considering stopping prescribed treatment. We do not advise on that, in the report or anywhere else. That conversation belongs with the doctor who prescribed it.
  • Your pain comes with anything that needs seeing today. Numbness around the saddle area, loss of bladder or bowel control, leg weakness that is getting worse, back pain with fever, or pain that wakes you every night and is worse lying down. These are not cases for a reading. Please be seen.
  • Recent injury, surgery or fragile bone. A fall or accident you have not had looked at, metalwork or a fusion from previous surgery, diagnosed osteoporosis, or blood thinners and bleeding disorders. Tell us at intake. Some of it rules out hands-on work entirely and all of it changes what can be written.
  • You are pregnant, or being treated for a serious condition. Not a refusal in itself, but tell us at intake. It changes what can responsibly be written.
